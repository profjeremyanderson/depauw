Academic integrity in exams
  • For exams given in class, you must write your answers solely from memory during the exam. No citing or quoting is required. You many not access books or notes of any kind, or collaborate with others, during an in-class exam.
  • For take-home exams (which we will have if an exam must be given remotely):
    • You may use any class materials (assigned texts; notes & other materials posted on Moodle or the linked Google Drive folders) and your own notes from class.
    • No other sources allowed. No collaboration with other people during the exam.
    • The academic integrity instructions will apply just as in out-of-class papers and reading responses. Cite all sources you use and put others’ words in quotation marks.


Info & questions for the early exam (Wednesday 9/23).

Questions are posted below to help you prepare.

The point of this exam is to encourage you to reflect on the material we have covered so far, and enhance your grasp of the most important parts: for jus ad bellum, what the criteria are and what they mean; for realism, pacifism, and just-war theory, what main conclusions different thinkers arrive at, and their main reasons for doing so. These matter because they form the background for much of what we will discuss for the rest of the course.

I will ask you to explain views from the areas we have studied. By "explain" I mean: don't attempt to provide every possibly relevant detail. There is not time for that. Instead, your task is to identify and articulate what is most important and distinctive about the views you explain.

On the other hand, it would not be good to try and boil each view down a mere few sentences. Aside from question #1, which is relatively straightforward, these views are too sophisticated to be summarized very briefly. You will have the entire class period, and I encourage you to use it to demonstrate how well you grasp what we've covered. I recommend you ensure you cover the core of each view you discuss, and then use any remaining time to add detail.

Integrity: No texts, notes, devices, or other aids allowed. You must write your essay with just your brains and hands. No citations or quoting required. I will provide blue books, and pens if you need to borrow one.



You must answer question #1. Then answer two – and only two -- of the other questions (#2, 3, or 4).


1. Criteria for jus ad bellum. Identify the eight criteria for jus ad bellum in the Cook reading. For each one, (a) briefly explain how it is supposed to constrain us from resorting to war and (b) discuss how it might be problematic.   


2. Realism. Here is a definition of realism: “Political leaders may, and will, do whatever they believe will maintain or increase their power. Morality plays no role in this, so rulers will ignore it. So, morality has no place in international relations, including in war.” Pick either the Athenian generals in Thucydides' Melian Debate, or Machiavelli (not both). For the one you choose, answer these questions:
  • To what extent do they (the Athenian generals, or Machiavelli) fit this definition of realism?
  • To what extent do they (the Athenian generals, or Machiavelli) depart from it?
Give examples from the text to support your answers. (Since the exam is in class you need not cite.)


3. Pacifism. We have discussed two defenses of anti-war pacifism: Lackey’s and Reader’s. Pick either Lackey or Reader (not both) and explain how he or she supports the view that it is morally wrong to go to war.
  • If you discuss Lackey, focus on section 5, "Antiwar Pacifism" (Lackey, 31-35). Ignore the rest.
  • If you discuss Reader, focus on sections 2 and 3, "The Presumption of War-ism" and "Harming the Enemy: Justifying Violence against Persons" (Reader, 171-8).

4. Criticism of pacifism or realism. We have read Anscombe’s critique of pacifism and Walzer’s critique of realism. Pick just one of them.
  • If you choose Anscombe, (a) explain her reasons for rejecting pacifism and (b) discuss how effective her reasons are against either Lackey or Reader (not both).
  • If you choose Walzer, (a) explain his reasons for rejecting realism and (b) discuss how effective his reasons are against either the Athenian generals or Machiavelli (not both).
